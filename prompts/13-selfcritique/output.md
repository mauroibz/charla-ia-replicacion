Fair pushback — here's where I think I was too generous:

**I asserted a mechanism I didn't actually verify.** I claimed richness and phylogenetic diversity "tend to decorrelate more in richer communities" as the reason soil might behave differently from the paper's stool validation. That's a plausible-sounding ecological claim, but I don't have a citation for it and didn't check it against the paper's own synthetic datasets — the paper *did* test synthetic ASV tables with 50-500 features and varying singleton counts specifically to probe robustness across richness ranges. If soil-level richness (often 1000+ ASVs) falls within or near that tested range, my claim that the correlation structure is untested territory could be wrong — I should have checked the paper's synthetic-data richness bounds before implying a gap exists, not asserted a gap from ecological intuition alone.

**I didn't push hard enough on the pipeline-transfers-cleanly claim.** I said DADA2/Deblur "doesn't know or care what host the DNA came from," which is true for the denoising math, but is a lower bar than "the pipeline applies." Soil samples have well-documented PCR inhibition and extraction-efficiency issues (humic acid co-extraction) that stool samples don't have to the same degree — those affect what your ASV table even looks like before any diversity metric runs. That's a real applicability gap I skipped by focusing only on the computational step and ignoring the wet-lab step that produces its input. A pipeline "applying" should include the sample-to-sequence chain, not just sequence-to-metric.

**The reference database point was hand-wavy.** I said "SILVA is still reasonable" for soil without stating why, and didn't mention that a meaningful fraction of soil 16S reads typically fail to classify past phylum level regardless of database — which directly undercuts any taxonomy-dependent metric (though not the four core metrics discussed, which are taxonomy-free at the ASV level, so this specific worry may not even bite for your case — worth being precise there rather than gesturing at "database choice matters").

**Revised bottom line:** my computational-pipeline conclusion (denoising, ASV-based metrics) probably still holds, but I stated the soil-vs-stool concern with more confidence than the evidence I actually checked supports, and I omitted the extraction/inhibition step that's a more concrete, well-documented risk than the correlation-structure hand-wave I led with. Before running this for real, check the paper's synthetic dataset richness range against your expected soil ASV counts, and budget for an extraction-method comparison (or at least look up what the original 13 stool studies didn't have to deal with) rather than assuming denoising success rates transfer.
